## Mock POC 3

- id: task-mock-poc-3
- url: phase7/task-mock-poc-3.html
- status: done
- description: Final POC certification run — you design the scenario, run the cycle near-solo, and defend the close under committee questioning.

### Learn

#### The formality check

<p>By now the POC process should be yours. Mock POC 3 flips the setup: <em>you</em> design the customer scenario (pick an industry and fleet mix the team actually sells into), your mentor plays it with minimal briefing, and the cycle runs with zero process coaching. New final element: you defend the close document under committee-style questioning — your mentor and one teammate playing the customer's IT director and CISO, pressing on results, gaps, and "why not the competitor?"</p>

#### What sign-off means

<p>Sign-off here certifies you to run real POCs solo — designing criteria with real customers, holding scope, triaging blockers, and closing with documents that survive committee scrutiny. If any process mark is still soft, this is the last cheap place to fix it; say so plainly at the retro.</p>

### Practice

- Design the scenario: industry, fleet, pains, one constraint your "customer" will reveal only if asked good discovery questions.
- Run the full cycle near-solo: kickoff, criteria, setup, check-ins, close.
- Convene the committee defense; field the pressure on results and gaps.
- Retro with your mentor: any soft process marks, named honestly.

## Complete 10 Onboarding Sessions Solo

- id: task-complete-10-onboarding-solo
- url: phase7/task-complete-10-onboarding-solo.html
- status: done
- description: Volume milestone — ten real onboarding sessions, alone, building the pattern library only reps can build.

### Learn

#### Why ten

<p>One good onboarding proves capability; ten build a <b>pattern library</b> — enough different admins, industries, and plumbing situations that new sessions start rhyming with old ones. Somewhere in the ten, the shift happens: from executing your runbook to improvising safely around it. That shift, not the count, is what this milestone is really measuring.</p>

#### Sustaining quality at volume

<p>The risks of reps are staleness and shortcut creep. Two guards: keep the pre-check ritual non-negotiable (it's precisely what shortcut creep eats first), and keep a one-line log per session — customer type, one thing that surprised you, one thing you'd redo. The log turns ten sessions into data; skim it at five and ten and feed anything structural back into the runbook for the whole team.</p>
<!-- PLACEHOLDER: confirm how sessions are assigned/tracked for this milestone and whether all ten must be fully solo or observer-permitted. -->

### Practice

- Confirm the assignment pipeline with your manager and get your first sessions scheduled.
- Run every session with full ritual: pre-checks, structure, written next steps, same-day notes.
- Keep the one-line log; review it at sessions five and ten.
- At ten: write the half-page "what ten onboardings taught me" and share it with the team.

### Quiz

1. The milestone's real measurement is…
   - [x] The shift from executing the runbook to improvising safely around it
   - [ ] The number ten itself
   - [ ] Speed per session
   - [ ] Customer size

2. Shortcut creep is countered by…
   - [x] Treating the pre-check ritual as non-negotiable regardless of confidence
   - [ ] More confidence
   - [ ] Fewer sessions
   - [ ] Longer meetings

3. The session log exists because…
   - [x] It converts reps into reviewable data and runbook improvements for the whole team
   - [ ] Managers count lines
   - [ ] Memory is reliable
   - [ ] Logs are billable

4. (text) From your log at session five: what's the most repeated surprise, and what runbook change does it suggest?

### Apply

- type: guided

Complete the ten, share the half-page findings, and propose one concrete runbook improvement to the team based on your log. Volume that doesn't flow back into shared practice is just mileage — this is where you start improving the mountain for the next climber.

## Sync with Sellers

- id: task-sync-with-sellers
- url: phase7/task-sync-with-sellers.html
- status: done
- description: Build your standing rhythm with the AEs you support — proactive deal syncs you drive, not meetings you attend.

### Learn

#### From resource to partner

<p>Until now, AEs pulled you into deals. The Phase 7 shift: you drive a standing sync with each aligned AE — short, regular, and yours to run. Agenda: deals in technical stages (your status, next actions), upcoming calls needing coverage, renewals wanting a pre-renewal technical touch, and anything you're seeing across deals worth their attention ("three prospects asked about conditional access this month").</p>

#### Running it well

<p>Fifteen minutes, your agenda sent beforehand, their calendar respected via Scheduling Assistant (full circle from Phase 1). Bring your pipeline reading, not just answers to theirs — the sync where you surface a stalled deal they'd deprioritized is the one that converts you from "technical resource" to "partner who moves my number."</p>
<!-- PLACEHOLDER: confirm which AEs each BTS engineer is aligned to and any existing sync cadence to slot into rather than duplicate. -->

### Practice

- Establish the sync with each aligned AE: cadence agreed, invite sent, agenda template shared.
- Before each: read the pipeline, prepare your deal statuses and one proactive observation.
- Run three cycles; track actions produced per sync.
- After three, ask each AE: "is this cadence and format worth your time?" — adjust.

### Quiz

1. The Phase 7 sync differs from earlier interactions because…

   - [ ] It's longer
   - [ ] The AE runs it
   - [ ] It's optional

2. The most partnership-building sync content is…
   - [x] Something you surfaced that the AE hadn't asked about
   - [ ] Perfect status recitations
   - [ ] Compliments
   - [ ] Product news

3. Asking "is this worth your time?" after three cycles…
   - [x] Keeps the sync earning its slot instead of surviving on politeness
   - [ ] Shows weakness
   - [ ] Is rhetorical

4. (text) What proactive observation have you brought to a sync, and what action came of it?

### Apply

- type: guided

Run the syncs for a month. Then ask one AE for the blunt version: what should you bring more of, less of? Their answer is your Phase 8 partnership polish list.

## Reverse Shadow All Calls

- id: task-reverse-shadow-all-calls
- url: phase7/task-reverse-shadow-all-calls.html
- status: done
- description: The roles flip — you run every call type while an observer watches silently and grades against the team bar.

### Learn

#### Reverse shadowing, defined

<p>In Phase 4 you shadowed; now the mirror: you run each call type — demo, onboarding, POC check-in, technical discovery — with a senior teammate observing silently and scoring against the team's bar. No net: the observer doesn't rescue, they record. It's the closest thing to an exam the expedition has, and it's meant to feel that way.</p>

#### Using the scores

<p>Ask each observer for structured output: strongest moment, weakest moment, one thing the team's best person does that you don't yet. Collect across all call types — the aggregate is your certification portfolio for Phase 8's go-live review, and the "not yet" list is your development plan for the first quarter after onboarding ends.</p>
<!-- PLACEHOLDER: confirm which call types require reverse shadow sign-off and whether one observer covers all or specialists each. -->

### Practice

- Schedule reverse shadows across every call type on the team's list.
- Run each with zero observer intervention expected or needed.
- Collect the structured feedback after each; no debate, just capture.
- Assemble the aggregate portfolio: scores, quotes, and your "not yet" development list.

### Quiz

1. Reverse shadowing differs from a mentored call because…
   - [x] The observer records rather than rescues — it's assessment, not support
   - [ ] It's shorter
   - [ ] The customer knows
   - [ ] There's no difference

2. The "one thing the best person does that you don't yet" question exists because…
   - [x] It converts assessment into a concrete post-onboarding development plan
   - [ ] Comparison is motivating
   - [ ] It fills the form
   - [ ] Best people need praise

3. The aggregate portfolio feeds…
   - [x] The Phase 8 go-live review
   - [ ] Your CV
   - [ ] The customer newsletter
   - [ ] Nothing

4. (text) Across your reverse shadows so far: what's the consistent strongest moment, and what's on the "not yet" list?

### Apply

- type: guided

Complete the full call-type list with clean scores. Where any call type scored soft, request one more observed rep before Phase 8 — walking into the final review with a known gap closed beats explaining it.
