## Meet the Team

- id: task-meet-the-team
- url: essentials/task-meet-the-team.html
- status: done
- description: Put names to faces — your manager, onboarding buddy, fellow BTS engineers, and the sellers you'll support every day.

### Learn

#### Who's who around you

<p>Your day-to-day orbit has four groups: your <b>manager</b> (priorities, feedback, sign-offs on each phase), your <b>onboarding buddy</b> (first stop for questions big and small), the <b>BTS team</b> (peers who run the same plays you're learning), and the <b>Account Executives / sellers</b> whose deals you'll provide technical coverage for.</p>
<!-- PLACEHOLDER: link team roster / org chart, and list actual names for manager, buddy, and aligned AEs -->

#### Why meet everyone early

<p>The BTS role runs on borrowed context: you'll constantly ask "has anyone seen this before?" Knowing who owns what — and having exchanged a first hello — makes every later ask easier. People help faster when you're a person, not a name in a channel.</p>

### Practice

- Ask your manager for the team roster and note each person's role.
- Introduce yourself to your onboarding buddy and agree how you'll communicate (Slack, calls, ad-hoc).
- Say hello to at least two BTS peers and one AE you'll support.
- Note each person's time zone and typical working hours.

### Quiz

1. Your first stop for everyday questions during onboarding is…
   - [ ] The customer
   - [x] Your onboarding buddy
   - [ ] IBM Support
   - [ ] Nobody — figure it out alone

2. Phase sign-offs and priorities come from…
   - [x] Your manager
   - [ ] The AE
   - [ ] Whoever is online
   - [ ] The customer success team

3. AEs / sellers rely on a BTS engineer for…
   - [ ] Payroll questions
   - [x] Technical coverage on their deals
   - [ ] Legal review
   - [ ] Office supplies

4. (text) Who are your manager, your buddy, and one AE you'll support — and what's one thing you learned about each?

### Apply

- type: guided

Set up short intro chats (15 minutes) with your buddy and one BTS peer this week. Come with two questions each: "what do you wish you'd known in week 1?" and "what does great BTS support look like from your seat?" Write down the answers — they become your working notes for Phase 1.

- url: essentials/task-enroll-laptop.html

#### What "enrolled" means

<p>Company laptops are managed devices: IT enrolls them into a management platform so security policies, disk encryption, updates, and required software arrive automatically. You'll live this same concept from the admin side later — MaaS360 does for customers exactly what IBM IT does for your laptop.</p>

#### What good setup looks like

### Practice

- Sign in to your corporate identity and confirm SSO works for at least one internal tool.
- Note the process a *customer's* employee would go through to enroll a device — you'll compare it to MaaS360 enrollment in Phase 2.

## Setup 1Password

- id: task-setup-1password
- url: essentials/task-setup-1password.html
- status: done
- description: Set up your password manager — vault, browser extension, and strong unique passwords from day one.

### Learn

#### Why a password manager

<p>You're about to create accounts on a dozen portals (MaaS360, ABM, KME, Salesloft, and more). A password manager generates and stores a strong, unique password for each so a breach of one never cascades. 1Password unlocks with one primary password — make that one long, memorable, and never reused.</p>
<!-- PLACEHOLDER: link IBM's 1Password enrollment/instructions page -->

#### Working habits

<p>Install the browser extension so credentials autofill on the right domain — this also protects against phishing look-alike sites, because autofill won't offer credentials on the wrong URL. Store portal credentials as you create them in later phases; never keep passwords in notes files or chat.</p>

### Practice

- Install 1Password (desktop + browser extension) and sign in / create your account per company instructions.
- Set a strong primary password you have never used anywhere else.
- Save your first credential and test autofill on that site.
- Turn on the app on your phone so passwords are available during demos and travel.

### Quiz

1. The main security win of a password manager is…
   - [x] A unique strong password per site, so one breach can't cascade
   - [ ] Not needing passwords at all
   - [ ] Sharing passwords with the team easily
   - [ ] Remembering one password for every site

2. Autofill from the browser extension also protects you because…
   - [x] It won't offer credentials on a look-alike phishing domain
   - [ ] It types faster
   - [ ] It disables cookies
   - [ ] It hides your IP address

3. Your primary (unlock) password should be…
   - [x] Long, memorable, and used nowhere else
   - [ ] Your email password
   - [ ] Written on a sticky note
   - [ ] Rotated daily

4. (text) Which portals do you expect to store credentials for during this onboarding? (Name at least three.)

### Apply

- type: guided

As you complete the rest of the Essentials tasks, store every new credential in 1Password at the moment you create it. By the end of Section 0 your vault — not your memory or a notes file — should be the only place your passwords exist.

## Setup Verify

- id: task-setup-verify
- url: essentials/task-setup-verify.html
- status: done
- description: Set up IBM Verify for multi-factor authentication so you can approve sign-ins from your phone.

### Learn

#### What Verify does

<p>IBM Verify is the MFA app you'll use to approve sign-ins to IBM systems — a push notification or one-time code on your phone proves it's really you, even if a password leaks. Most internal tools will prompt it regularly, so it's worth setting up properly on day one.</p>
<!-- PLACEHOLDER: link IBM Verify registration instructions for new hires -->

#### MFA in one breath

<p>MFA combines something you <em>know</em> (password) with something you <em>have</em> (your phone). This is the same class of protection you'll later discuss with customers — identity and conditional access are recurring themes in MaaS360 conversations, and having lived it makes it easy to explain.</p>

### Practice

- Install IBM Verify on your phone and register it against your IBM id per instructions.
- Test one sign-in end-to-end and confirm the push/code flow works.
- Set up the backup/recovery method offered during registration so a lost phone doesn't lock you out.
- Store any recovery codes in 1Password.

### Quiz

1. MFA protects you because an attacker with your password still lacks…
   - [x] The second factor — your enrolled device
   - [ ] Your email address
   - [ ] Your username
   - [ ] Your browser history

2. The two factors in "password + Verify push" are…
   - [x] Something you know + something you have
   - [ ] Two passwords
   - [ ] Two devices
   - [ ] A password + a security question

3. If you lose the phone with Verify on it, you can still get in because…
   - [x] You set up a backup/recovery method during registration
   - [ ] MFA turns off automatically
   - [ ] IT can read your mind
   - [ ] You memorized the push notifications

4. (text) Where else have you seen MFA in daily life, and how would you explain its value to a non-technical customer in one sentence?

### Apply

- type: guided

Do one full real sign-in with Verify, then explain to your buddy in under a minute what happened behind the scenes (password checked, push sent to enrolled device, approval completes the session). That one-minute version is a building block for later identity conversations with customers.

## Setup Passkey

- id: task-setup-passkey
- url: essentials/task-setup-passkey.html
- status: done
- description: Register a passkey for passwordless, phishing-resistant sign-in where IBM systems support it.

### Learn

#### What a passkey is

<p>A passkey replaces a password with a cryptographic key pair: the private key stays on your device (unlocked by fingerprint, face, or device PIN) and the service holds only the public key. There's nothing to type, reuse, or steal in a database breach.</p>

#### Why it resists phishing

<p>A passkey is bound to the real website's domain. A look-alike phishing site can't request it — the browser simply won't offer it — which kills the most common attack on passwords outright. Expect passkeys to come up in customer identity conversations too; being able to explain them plainly is a small superpower.</p>
<!-- PLACEHOLDER: link IBM's passkey registration instructions and note which systems support it -->

### Practice

- Register a passkey following the instructions for your IBM id (laptop biometric, phone, or security key as directed).
- Sign in once using the passkey instead of your password.
- Confirm your fallback method still works (password + Verify) in case you switch devices.
- Explain to your buddy in two sentences why a passkey can't be phished.

### Quiz

1. With a passkey, the service you sign in to stores…
   - [x] Only your public key — useless to an attacker on its own
   - [ ] Your private key
   - [ ] Your fingerprint image
   - [ ] Your password, encrypted

2. A phishing site can't harvest your passkey because…
   - [x] The passkey is bound to the real domain, so the browser won't offer it elsewhere
   - [ ] Phishing sites can't load JavaScript
   - [ ] Passkeys expire hourly
   - [ ] It can — passkeys don't help with phishing

3. Your biometric (fingerprint/face) is used to…
   - [x] Unlock the private key locally on your device
   - [ ] Get uploaded to IBM's servers
   - [ ] Replace your username
   - [ ] Encrypt your email

4. (text) In one sentence each: how would you explain a passkey to a customer's CISO, and to a non-technical end user?

### Apply

- type: guided

Use your passkey as the default sign-in for a full day. Note anywhere you were forced back to password + MFA — that map of "what supports passkeys vs what doesn't" mirrors what customers experience rolling out modern auth.

## i9 Forms

- id: task-i9-forms
- url: essentials/task-i9-forms.html
- status: done
- description: Complete Form I-9 employment eligibility verification — Section 1 on or before day one, identity documents shortly after.

### Learn

#### What the I-9 is

<p>Form I-9 verifies your identity and authorization to work in the United States — every US employee completes one. You fill in <b>Section 1</b> (personal attestation) no later than your first day of work, and present acceptable original documents so the employer can complete its section within the required window (three business days of your start date).</p>

#### Documents that work

<p>You either present one <b>List A</b> document proving both identity and work authorization (e.g. a US passport or permanent resident card), or a combination of one <b>List B</b> identity document (e.g. driver's license) plus one <b>List C</b> work-authorization document (e.g. Social Security card or birth certificate). Originals are required — photocopies generally are not accepted.</p>
<!-- PLACEHOLDER: link the internal HR portal / instructions for where and how IBM collects I-9 and whether remote verification applies -->

### Practice

- Complete Section 1 in the HR system on or before your first day.
- Check the List A / List B+C options and gather the original documents you'll present.
- Follow the HR instructions for document verification (in person or remote, as directed).
- Confirm in the HR portal that your I-9 shows complete.

### Quiz

1. Section 1 of the I-9 must be completed…
   - [x] No later than your first day of work
   - [ ] Within your first month
   - [ ] Only if you're a contractor
   - [ ] After your first paycheck

2. A US passport on its own is sufficient because…
   - [x] It's a List A document proving both identity and work authorization
   - [ ] It's a List B document
   - [ ] Passports aren't accepted
   - [ ] It only proves identity

3. A driver's license alone is not enough because…
   - [x] It proves identity (List B) but not work authorization — you also need a List C document
   - [ ] It's not a real ID
   - [ ] Licenses expire
   - [ ] It is enough

4. (text) Which document option are you using, and is anything blocking you from completing verification on time?

### Apply

- type: guided

Complete the I-9 end to end this week. If any document is missing or expired, flag it to HR immediately rather than waiting — the three-business-day window is a legal requirement, not a guideline.
