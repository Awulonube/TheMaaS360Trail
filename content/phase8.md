## Review + Q&A

- id: task-review-qa
- url: phase8/task-review-qa.html
- status: done
- description: The full-mountain review — every layer from Basecamp up, tested in one structured Q&A session.

### Learn

#### The shape of the review

<p>One session, your manager and mentor, covering the whole climb: the 101/201/301 platform ladders (Apple, Android, Windows), identity, MTD, groups and policy targeting, the troubleshooting method, and the craft layers — demo, POC, onboarding. The format is Q&A, not presentation: they probe, you answer cold. Anything you built along the way (health checks, runbook, POC kit, decision tables) may be opened and challenged.</p>

#### How to prepare without cramming

<p>Don't re-read everything; test yourself instead. The high-yield drill: have your buddy fire the classic hard questions across every layer — APNs renewal gone wrong, mode-mix design, conditional access qualifying, proportionate device actions, scope-pressure handling — and answer out loud. Where you reach for notes, that's the re-study list. Fluency under questioning, not coverage, is what the session measures.</p>

### Practice

- Build the self-test list: two hard questions per layer, fired by your buddy, answered cold.
- Re-study only what the self-test exposed.
- Refresh your artifacts — health checks, runbook, POC kit — so any opened document is current.
- Schedule and complete the review session.

### Quiz

1. The review format is Q&A because…
   - [x] Fluency under questioning is the job's real condition, and the session measures it
   - [ ] Presentations take longer
   - [ ] Slides are banned
   - [ ] It's easier to run

2. Preparation should center on…
   - [x] Self-testing out loud and re-studying only what it exposes
   - [ ] Re-reading every card
   - [ ] Memorizing quiz answers
   - [ ] Nothing

3. Artifacts are refreshed beforehand because…
   - [x] Any of them may be opened and challenged live
   - [ ] They expire

4. (text) What did your self-test expose, and how did the review session actually go on those items?

### Apply

- type: guided

Complete the review. Capture the two or three questions that stretched you furthest — they go on the development list, and they're also your preview of what real customers' hardest evaluators sound like.

## Reverse Shadow All Calls

- id: task-reverse-shadow-all-calls
- url: phase8/task-reverse-shadow-all-calls.html
- status: done
- description: The final observed pass — one last reverse shadow on each call type, closing any score left soft in Phase 7.

### Learn

#### The closing pass

<p>Phase 7's reverse shadows produced your score portfolio and possibly some soft spots. This final pass is targeted: one more observed rep on each call type that needs it — at minimum, one demo and one onboarding even if all scores were clean, as the formal closing evidence for go-live. Same rules: silent observer, structured scores, no rescues.</p>

#### Done means done

<p>The exit condition is explicit: every call type on the team's list carries a clean reverse-shadow score recent enough to trust. When this pass ends, the observation era ends with it — the next person watching your calls will be a new hire shadowing <em>you</em>. Worth pausing on: the mountain you climbed becomes the one you'll guide others up, and your Phase 4 observation sheet is about to get reused from the other side.</p>

### Practice

- List call types with their Phase 7 scores; identify what needs a closing rep.
- Schedule the targeted reverse shadows, including the formal demo and onboarding passes.
- Run them clean; collect the final structured scores.
- File the completed portfolio for the go-live review.

### Quiz

1. This final pass targets…
   - [x] Soft scores from Phase 7, plus formal closing passes on demo and onboarding
   - [ ] Every call type again from scratch
   - [ ] Only your favorite call type
   - [ ] Nothing specific

2. The exit condition is…
   - [x] Every call type carrying a clean, recent reverse-shadow score
   - [ ] Ten more sessions
   - [ ] A feeling of readiness
   - [ ] Manager availability

3. After this pass, the observer role…
   - [x] Flips — new hires will shadow you
   - [ ] Continues indefinitely
   - [ ] Moves to the customer
   - [ ] Disappears from the team

4. (text) What was your final soft spot, and what closed it?

### Apply

- type: guided

Complete the closing passes and file the portfolio. Then dig out your Phase 4 observation sheet and put it somewhere you'll find it — the next new hire's first shadow request is closer than you think.

## Go Live Readiness Check

- id: task-go-live-readiness
- url: phase8/task-go-live-readiness.html
- status: done
- description: The summit — final sign-off, full solo scope, and the transition from onboarding to the job itself.

### Learn

#### The final gate

<p>One meeting: your manager, your portfolio, and the go-live decision. The evidence is already assembled — review session outcome, complete reverse-shadow portfolio, solo volume record, POC certification, AE sync rhythm. What's decided here: full solo scope confirmed, your name in the regular assignment rotation, and the support agreement replaced by the normal team structures everyone works within (peer review, escalation paths, the weekly call).</p>

#### After the summit

<p>Three things worth doing in week one post-certification: set your development plan from the accumulated "not yet" list with a first check-in date; volunteer for one new-hire support role (buddy, shadow host, or runbook owner) — teaching locks in what you learned; and send a short thank-you to the people who ran your nets. The expedition ends; the role begins.</p>

### Practice

- Assemble the final portfolio: every phase's evidence in one place.
- Confirm outstanding items are genuinely closed (not "mostly").
- Hold the go-live meeting; get the explicit scope and rotation confirmation.
- Set the post-certification development plan and its first check-in date.

### Quiz

1. Go-live means…
   - [x] Full solo scope, the regular rotation, and normal team structures replacing onboarding nets
   - [ ] No more learning
   - [ ] Working alone forever
   - [ ] A title change only

2. The development plan exists because…
   - [x] The "not yet" list doesn't vanish at certification — growth just stops being scaffolded
   - [ ] Plans are mandatory
   - [ ] It's ceremonial
   - [ ] Managers like documents

3. Volunteering for a new-hire support role is suggested because…
   - [x] Teaching locks in learning — and someone ran your nets
   - [ ] It's required
   - [ ] It pays extra
   - [ ] The rotation demands it

4. (text) What's the first item on your post-certification development plan, and when's the first check-in?

### Apply

- type: guided

Hold the meeting. Get the sign-off. Update your Slack status to something that isn't "onboarding." Peak Certified isn't the end of the mountain — it's the height from which you can finally see the whole range. Congratulations, and go take the first call of the rest of the job.
